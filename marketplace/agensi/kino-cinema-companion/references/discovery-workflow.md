# Discovery workflow

1. Capture the task's mood, desired intensity, runtime window, format, language, release period, and content exclusions. Do not ask for details that are optional to a useful first answer.
2. Summarize the relevant supplied preference signals in a short private working note. Never transmit that note or raw history to an external source without explicit consent.
3. Build candidate titles from available research/catalog tools or user-supplied candidates. Search candidates independently of the personal history where possible.
4. Verify every factual field used in the explanation (identity, format, year, genre/theme, runtime, language, country, availability) against a recognizable source. Prefer primary/catalog sources; cite source and access date when current facts matter.
5. Exclude watched titles, apply constraints, and choose a mix of strong/probable/exploratory options. Keep a smaller list when evidence is thin.
6. Present: title and type; verified year if useful; qualitative tier; why it fits; any caveat; citation for researched facts.

When no research tool is available, say the metadata is unverified and rely only on durable, high-confidence knowledge without specific uncertain claims. Streaming availability should be omitted unless current and region-specific evidence is available.
